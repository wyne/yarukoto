---
title: A daily workflow
description: How the Inbox, Today and Plan tabs fit together, from writing a task down to planning the weeks ahead.
---

Yarukoto doesn't make you work any particular way, but its tabs are laid out in the order a task
usually travels: it's written down in the **Inbox**, given a day, done from **Today**, and
anything that needs more thought than a swipe is placed on a calendar in **Plan**. This page walks
through that progression and the habits that keep it light.

## 1. Capture everything into the Inbox

The Inbox holds every task that isn't in a list yet. It's the one place you never have to decide
anything: when something comes to mind, add it there and get back to what you were doing.

- On a phone, tap **+** and type. On a computer, press **⌘N** on the Mac, or click the add field
  at the top of the list.
- Write it the way you'd say it. Quick add picks out the details and leaves the rest as the title:

  | Type | Sets |
  |---|---|
  | `today`, `tomorrow` or `tmrw`, a weekday such as `fri` | The due date |
  | `6pm` or `18:30`, alongside a date | The due time |
  | `#home` | A tag |
  | `~groceries` | The list |
  | `!high`, `!med`, `!low` | The priority |
  | `every monday` | A repeat |

  So `call the dentist fri 9am !high` is a high-priority task due Friday at 9 AM. Everything is
  optional: a bare `call the dentist` is a perfectly good Inbox entry.
- Where you add a task decides its defaults. In a list, it goes into that list. On **Today**, it's
  due today. On **Plan**, it's due on the day you've selected. In the Inbox, it has no list
  and no date until you give it one.

The point of the Inbox is speed, so resist filling in every detail while capturing. Triage is a
separate step, and it goes faster when you do a batch at once.

## 2. Triage with a swipe

Open the Inbox once or twice a day and decide what each task is for. On iPhone and Android, swipe
a task to the left for three buttons:

- **Today:** it's happening today and will show up in Today.
- **Tmrw:** not today, but soon.
- **Next week:** a week from today. Use it for anything that can wait, so it comes back on its
  own instead of sitting in front of you.

On a computer, right-click a task for the same three dates, or press **⌘T** for today and **⌥⌘T**
for tomorrow with the task selected. Select several tasks first (⇧↑ ⇧↓, or ⇧-click) to schedule
them all at once.

Triage is also when a task finds its list. Open it and pick one, use **⇧⌘M** on the Mac, or type
`~list` when you add it. Filing is what takes a task out of the Inbox: a task you've only given a
date stays there, now with its date beside it, until it has a list. So a full triage gives each
task both, a day for when and a list for what it's part of. A task that doesn't belong to a day yet,
like an idea or something you're waiting on, can go into a list with no date at all.

## 3. Work from Today

**Today** is the list to keep open while you work. It shows everything due today, plus anything
whose date has passed, so a task you didn't get to rolls forward on its own and is never quietly
lost.

- Check tasks off as you go. What you finish today collects under **Completed** at the bottom, so
  you can see what you've done.
- If something isn't going to happen today, swipe it to **Tmrw** or **Next week** rather than
  letting it go overdue. An honest Today is a short one.
- Tasks added while you're on Today are due today, so it doubles as a scratch list for the day.

On a Mac, the menu bar icon shows today's count and lists today's tasks: choose one to open it, or
hold Option to check it off without opening the window. Windows has the same in the notification area.

## 4. Plan the days ahead

Swiping is for one task at a time. When you have a pile to spread out, or want to think about the
week rather than the day, open the **Plan** tab. It puts the tasks that still need a day beside
a calendar, and you drag them onto the days they belong to.

- **On a phone**, tap **Plan task** at the bottom of Plan. A sheet opens with every task
  that has no date or is overdue, highest priority first. Long-press one, and the sheet gets out of
  the way so you can drag it onto any day. Tap a few tasks first to carry them together.
- **On a computer**, the same tasks sit in a pane beside the calendar. Drag them onto a day in the
  month grid, or onto a column in the 2-day, 3-day or week view.

Tasks already on the calendar can be dragged too: drag one to another day to move it, or within a
day to change its order.

[Planning with the Plan tab](/using/plan/) covers the views, the filters and the gestures in
detail.

## Making it a habit

A rhythm that works well with these tabs:

- **Through the day:** capture into the Inbox without stopping to organize.
- **Once a day,** often first thing or at the end of the day: empty the Inbox with swipes, then look
  at Today and push what won't happen to tomorrow.
- **Once a week:** open Plan in the week view, work through the overdue and undated tasks in
  the side pane, and drop each onto a day. Anything you can't place is a sign it belongs in a list
  rather than on the calendar, or doesn't need doing.

Some other habits that help:

- **Use priorities sparingly.** Plan's task pane sorts by priority, so keeping `!high`
  for what really matters puts those tasks at the top when you plan.
- **Give dates, not just lists.** A task in a list with no date never reaches Today. If it should
  happen, give it a day, even a rough one; dragging it to a different day later is easy.
- **Use repeats for routines.** `water the plants every sat ~house` files and schedules itself, so
  routines never need triage.
- **Save a filter for recurring reviews.** In Browse, a filter like "#work, due this week" can be
  saved and reopened from the sidebar.
